Both models are run the way a user would run them: from each site's soil file and each treatment's revised vege file, on daily climate, with no soil water resets and no measured cover. The original model uses the soil files as they were. The new model carries the improvements agreed in November 2026, fitted jointly on cumulative observed vs predicted totals.

What changed and why it matters

  • Runoff: the original soil files overpredicted runoff from every treatment except zero till by 18–41% and underpredicted zero till by 18–26%. With HowLeaky AMC, a shared CN–cover reduction and the zero-till offset, all 10 catchments sit within 7% of unity. Wallumbilla Min till is the weakest on timing (NSE 0.42).
  • Soil movement: the PERFECT curve with soil-file K overpredicted the covered treatments up to 2.1× at Greenwood. The exponential curve brings all eight within 0.71–1.19. Greenwood Burnt (0.71) is held down by one intense storm (7 February 1981).
  • Water quality: suspended sediment is now modelled directly, with one shared cover response and a site-specific bare-soil concentration.
  • Soil water: PAW improves at Greenwood (RMSE 50–62 mm, from 76–94) and is about level at Greenmount. It is worse at Wallumbilla (54–56 mm, from 41), where the vege file's no-stress threshold of 0.2 leaves the profile too wet. That is the crop water-use work still to do.
  • Inputs matter as much as the model: with the original vege files, the same new model gave runoff P:O 0.89–1.16 and soil movement 0.69–1.49. Redrawing fallow residue from measured cover closed most of that gap.
